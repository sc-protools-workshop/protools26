- 9:05 - 10:00 am<br>
    *Invited Talk*: HPC means Heterogeneity, Performance and Complexity<br>
    Estela Suarez, Juelich Supercomputing Centre<br>
    *Abstract*: The variety of applications using or seeking to use high-performance computing (HPC) resources is growing, as is their demand for computing power and data management. Meanwhile, the physical limits of Moore's law are prompting the development of new computing solutions, including a growing range of custom-designed processing units and accelerators, as well as emerging techniques such as quantum and neuromorphic computing. Data centres and HPC hosting sites face the challenge of designing systems that can meet the needs of diverse applications, integrate different processing technologies and minimize energy consumption and operational costs. This is resulting in increasingly heterogeneous system designs comprising multiple partitions or modules with different node architectures. However, such diversity also increases the complexity on the side of system operation, parallel programming, performance analysis, and application optimization.
    This talk reviews current trends in the HPC landscape, highlighting key challenges and opportunities for the HPC/AI community.

- 2:00pm - 3:00pm <br>
    *Invited Talk*: Accelerating HPC Performance Analysis with AI Agents<br>
    Kevin Huck, AMD<br>
    Successful performance analysis typically involves a combination of established workflows, measurement tools, architectural knowledge, performance models, hardware counter analysis, parametric studies, and visualization. AI agents can integrate and automate many of these activities, significantly reducing the effort required to collect, organize, analyze, and interpret performance data. Agents are particularly effective at automating command-line data collection, managing results from parametric studies, applying performance models to known architectures, interpreting well understood hardware counter data, and performing static analysis of compiler reports and generated assembly code. In this talk, we will present several success stories that demonstrate how AI agents can be used to analyze HPC proxy applications and accelerate the performance engineering process. For example, analysis of the RAJAPerf suite on AMD MI300A GPUs identified optimization opportunities that produced a 34× speedup in one kernel. We will also discuss practical lessons learned, including common challenges and limitations encountered when working with AI agents. We will also discuss emerging opportunities for agent-assisted performance analysis, including the skills, interfaces, and rules that performance measurement and analysis tool developers should consider providing to maximize agent effectiveness. Finally, we will highlight AI agent capabilities being developed for current and future releases of the ROCm ecosystem.
